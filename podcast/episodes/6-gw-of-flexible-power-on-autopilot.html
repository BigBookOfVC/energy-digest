About the episode

Jürgen Mayerhofer is co-founder and CEO of enspired, a fully automated platform that monetises flexible assets on Europe’s short-term power markets. A software engineer with two decades in physical energy, he was head of trading automation at Trayport and MD of Visotech, the algorithmic trading vendor he sold in 2019.

The DNA traces back to the end of 2014, when EPEX opened its M7 API and Jürgen’s team built some of Europe’s first certified algorithmic trading software. What began as pushing one order without a human became algorithms running thousands of trades a day. enspired now carries that instinct through the whole company: an asset owner hands over a battery, a pumped storage plant or a CHP unit, and it is connected, traded in milliseconds and reported back on a live dashboard. There is no shift trading team and no desk of twelve screens.

The counter-intuitive thread is that energy is a bad fit for AI in the way most people assume. The problem is not too much data but too little, and what exists is contaminated by nuclear phase-outs, an energy crisis, COVID demand destruction and geopolitical shocks. Ask Jürgen for the real bottleneck and he does not say models or compute. He says culture.

  • The conversation covers:
  • The small-data problem: ancillary services clear in four-hour blocks, so three years of history is barely a handful of usable points.
  • Why twenty years of price history tells you nothing about a battery in 2026, unlike gold or any other commodity.
  • Why a battery is the hardest problem in reinforcement learning, against AlphaGo’s fixed board and fixed rules.
  • The three structural bottlenecks in European energy trading: data, culture, and software engineering talent.
  • Why traders resist automation on incentives rather than technology, and why build-it-in-house takes eighteen months to fail.
  • The founding bet: more renewables structurally widen the very spreads that flexibility traders live on.
  • Hyperscalers discovering you cannot buy a grid connection with a valuation, and real-time pricing of compute as unclaimed optionality.
  • Why the best EV smart-charging idea died on incentives and metering regulation, and who is actually executing on it.
  • Europe as a first-mover marching game, the US as a volume play, and why Japan fits the European model best.
  • What happens when more batteries compress the spreads, and why the free market self-corrects before the alpha disappears.
  • Publishing KPMG-audited monthly performance, and why eighteen months later nobody has followed.
