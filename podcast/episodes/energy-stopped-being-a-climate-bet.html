About the episode

Jan Lozek is co-founder and Managing Director of Future Energy Ventures, the Berlin-based platform that backs the intelligence layer of the energy transition. He set up the first fund in 2016 with RWE, a €300M vehicle that made 50 investments; the second fund closed at €235M. Before that he spent years inside the utilities, at RWE and E.ON.

The thesis has not moved since 2016: the hardware is largely solved, and the value migrates to the software that connects, orchestrates and monetises it. What changed is why anyone cares. For a decade energy was sold to LPs as a climate story. Now it is a sovereignty trade. Europe spends roughly €1.6 trillion importing fossil fuels, and every price shock of the last two decades has been a reminder of the exposure.

From there the conversation splits the US and Europe apart, then puts them back together: two opposite narratives, one identical bottleneck. It ends on the sharpest idea in the episode, which is that owning infrastructure is not a venture case but privileged access to it is.

  • The conversation covers:
  • Why the energy transition became a defensive sovereignty strategy rather than a climate story.
  • The same grid problem in the US and Europe, wrapped in two opposite narratives: one solving for AI demand, the other for independence.
  • Why centralised versus distributed is the wrong debate, and why the value sits in the software between them.
  • Why owning infrastructure is not a venture case, but institutionalised access to it is.
  • The efficiency hiding inside the US grid as pure software upside, cheaper than building new.
  • Two overlooked bets: robotics for energy infrastructure, driven by demographics, and industrial decarbonisation.
  • What China taught Europe about hardware, and the two-track response of a strategic reserve plus buying the best.
  • Why team composition remains the single biggest reason companies fail, and why FEV keeps a psychologist on the platform.
  • What utilities look like in ten years, and the one thing they will never be good at.
