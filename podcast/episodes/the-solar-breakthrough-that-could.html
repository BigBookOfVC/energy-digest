About the episode

The US energy demand curve has gone vertical. Data centres, AI training runs, electrification and manufacturing reshoring are all pulling on the grid at once, while interconnection queues stretch into the next decade and the political argument stays stuck on nuclear restarts and gas peakers.

In this episode Rohit Yadav sits down with Scott Wharton, CEO of Tandem PV, whose 65,000 square foot Fremont factory opened this year. Tandem’s bet is not to replace silicon but to stack on top of it: a perovskite layer roughly one micron thick, about a hundredth of a human hair, coated onto the top glass of an otherwise conventional panel. The cheaper silicon gets, the better their economics get.

The conversation moves from that chemistry out to the things that actually govern deployment: where solar cost really sits, why Texas is now outbuilding California, what Solyndra should have taught the sector about sequencing capital, and why the claim that America has forgotten how to manufacture does not survive contact with the Bay Area talent pool.

  • The conversation covers:
  • Don’t fight the commodity, ride on top of it: why head-on silicon replacement is the strategy that keeps killing solar startups.
  • About 75–80% of a US utility-scale deployment’s cost is land, labour, permitting and balance of system, not the panel.
  • Why efficiency, not panel price, is the lever that attacks the whole cost stack, and why that matters most where interconnection is the bottleneck.
  • Texas is deploying roughly three times as much solar as California this year, because deregulation made it faster to build.
  • The funding discipline lesson: match each round to a specific de-risking milestone rather than jumping from R&D to a gigafab.
  • Why the “Americans can’t manufacture” myth is wrong, and what the Fremont factory proves.
