About the episode

Arjun Gupta founded Smart Joules to sell something nobody in India was selling. Not audits, not recommendations, but energy savings themselves, with the company carrying the capital and the risk and taking a share of what the customer saved. Everyone told him the proposition was excellent. Nobody signed for a year and a half. The first customer turned out to be a hospital he could walk to, the savings came in at three times the model, and the first cheque arrived at three or four times what the team had expected.

What follows is a story about incentives rather than technology. The early customers were single-site owners who left the engineering alone. The large brands that came next had their own teams, processes and approvals, and the same work took five times as long. The breakthrough was a portfolio contract with Apollo covering 20 hospitals over ten years, which tripled in about a year a business that had taken six years to build. Then came the harder realisation: efficiency is a vitamin in a market obsessed with growth, so Smart Joules stopped selling efficiency and started selling cooling, the largest single end use of energy in India, on long-term service contracts.

The AI conversation starts from an unusual position, which is ten years of minute-level operating data from chillers in 28 cities. Arjun contrasts that with the ten-point design curve a manufacturer hands you, and the roughly 50 percent gap between design and in-field performance. The same data now sits inside the company’s own building management system, in a market where only ten of every hundred buildings buy a BMS at all and only one of those ten actually works. And the 60 chiller plants under management are starting to look less like consumption and more like dispatchable load.
